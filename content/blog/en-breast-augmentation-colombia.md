There is no official ranking of the **best breast augmentation in Colombia**, so the useful question is how to tell a well-qualified plastic surgeon and facility from a well-marketed one. For U.S. patients researching **breast augmentation colombia**, three checks usually matter most: verify the surgeon and the facility in Colombia’s official registries, confirm in consultation that implants or fat transfer actually fit your anatomy and goals, and build the trip around recovery—not vacation photos. One number often shapes the calendar: Avianca, Colombia’s flag carrier, lists travel **14 days** after mammoplasty (augmentation or reduction) or breast pexy, with a medical certificate.

This guide walks through those checks, Cartagena logistics, and the questions worth bringing to a consult.

## What “best breast augmentation in Colombia” should actually mean

Rankings and “top clinic” lists are mostly advertising. These are the checks that hold up before you buy a flight:

**1. Specialist registration.** Look the surgeon up in ReTHUS, the Colombian Ministry of Health’s national registry of health professionals, and confirm plastic surgery is listed as the specialty—not only a general medical degree.
**2. Society membership, if claimed.** Confirm membership in the [Colombian Society of Plastic, Aesthetic and Reconstructive Surgery (SCCP)](https://www.cirugiaplastica.org.co/) on its own directory.
**3. Facility authorization.** Get the exact name of the clinic or hospital and check it in REPS, the Ministry’s registry of authorized health providers, including that surgical services are enabled at that site.
**4. Anesthesia.** Confirm an anesthesiologist provides anesthesia in an operating room.
**5. Implant and follow-up plan in writing.** Ask for implant manufacturer/model/warranty and device tracking, how many in-person post-op visits happen before you fly, and how remote follow-up works once you are back in the U.S.

Red flags: a surgery date offered before anyone reviews your history and photos, guaranteed cup sizes or “cloned” results, pressure to combine many procedures “while you are here,” or advice to book a return flight within a few days.

## What breast augmentation is designed to address

Breast augmentation (augmentation mammaplasty) increases breast volume using implants or, in selected cases, fat transfer. According to the [American Society of Plastic Surgeons (ASPS)](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-augmentation), it can restore volume lost after weight change or pregnancy, improve shape balance, or address asymmetry—but it does **not** correct severe drooping on its own; a lift may be needed in the same or a separate operation.

Implant choice involves fill material, shape, profile, and size matched to chest width, tissue thickness, and goals. Placement (above or below the muscle) and incision location are clinical decisions—not catalogue picks. No reputable surgeon can promise a cloned photo result.

In consultation, Dr. Fulvio Correa—a *Miembro de Número* of the Colombian Society of Plastic Surgery (SCCP)—reviews medical history, pregnancy plans, imaging needs when relevant, skin quality, asymmetry, and lifestyle. That evaluation determines whether implants, fat transfer, a lift, or waiting is appropriate.

## Who is usually a candidate—and who should wait

Strong candidacy conversations usually cover stable weight, smoking status, medications that affect clotting, prior breast surgery, and whether pregnancy is planned soon. If major weight change is still underway, or if you cannot arrange help for the first days after surgery, many surgeons prefer to delay rather than rush.

Be ready to discuss mammogram history, family breast-cancer risk when relevant, and how much time you can realistically take away from work and childcare.

## How to evaluate a surgeon and facility (checklist)

Before committing to travel, verify:

• Specialist training in plastic surgery and current registration in ReTHUS
• SCCP membership when claimed—confirm on official listings
• Where surgery is performed (a facility authorized in REPS for surgical services) and who provides anesthesia
• Implant brand, model, warranty documentation, and device tracking
• How many in-person post-op visits are planned before clearance to fly
• How remote follow-up works after you return to the U.S. (photos, video, messaging channel)
• Written guidance on support bras/garments, activity limits, and warning signs

Ask for a clear explanation of risks that apply to breast implants and surgery generally—including anesthesia risks, bleeding, infection, seroma, capsular contracture, implant malposition or rupture over time, changes in sensation, unfavorable scarring, and the possibility of future revision—as summarized in [ASPS breast augmentation safety](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-augmentation/safety). Avoid anyone who guarantees a specific look or downplays recovery.

## Medical travel logistics that usually matter

Build your itinerary around clinical milestones, not vacation days. Ask how early you should arrive to complete pending labs or evaluations and settle into lodging near follow-up care—timing is case-specific. Keep return flights flexible until your surgical team clears you.

Airlines add their own rules on top of medical clearance. [Avianca’s help center](https://ayuda.avianca.com/hc/en-us/articles/13090597036315-Are-there-any-travel-restrictions-for-passengers-with-special-medical-conditions) lists travel from **14 days** after mammoplasty (augmentation or reduction) or breast pexy (with or without implants), and asks for a medical certificate issued no more than 10 days before the flight. Check your own carrier’s policy before booking.

A companion helps with walking, meals, garment changes, and clinic visits in the first days. Choose lodging with an elevator when possible, reliable air conditioning, and a quiet room. ## Recovery themes to confirm with your surgeon

[ASPS recovery guidance](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-augmentation/recovery) notes that breasts are typically wrapped or supported with an elastic bandage or support bra to minimize swelling, that acute pain often eases within about one to five days, and that soreness and swelling can last a few weeks—with exercise and normal activity resumed only as your surgeon directs. Final appearance continues to settle for months (“drop and fluff” language online is informal, not a clinical schedule).

Ask how mammograms and future breast imaging are handled after implants, and how you should monitor for changes once you are back in the United States. Do not use social-media timelines as medical clearance.

## Questions worth bringing to a virtual or in-person consult

• Based on my measurements, which implant dimensions are realistic—and which are not?
• Implant vs. fat transfer: which fits my tissue and goals?
• Do I also need a lift, now or later?
• Where would scars typically sit, and how are they followed?
• What implant brand/model would you use, and how is the device tracked?
• How many nights near the clinic should I plan before considering a return flight?
• What is the plan if a complication appears after I fly home?
• Who is my after-hours contact while I am still in Colombia?
