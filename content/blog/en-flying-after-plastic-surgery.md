There is no single safe number of days for **flying after plastic surgery**, but if you are flying home from Colombia there is a firm floor to plan around. Avianca, Colombia's flag carrier, lists **10 to 15 days** after cosmetic surgery depending on the procedure (14 days for breast surgery, liposuction, and tummy tuck; 15 days when two or more body procedures are combined) and requires a medical certificate issued within 10 days of the flight. That airline minimum sits on top of your surgeon's clearance, not in place of it.

So the practical answer to **how long after plastic surgery can you fly** is: the later of two dates, the airline's minimum and the day your surgical team examines you and signs off.

## The airline minimum, procedure by procedure

Avianca publishes waiting periods for passengers with medical conditions, based on the guide from Colombia's civil aviation authority (Aerocivil). For the procedures most often planned by travelers coming to Cartagena, its [help center](https://ayuda.avianca.com/hc/en-us/articles/13090597036315-Are-there-any-travel-restrictions-for-passengers-with-special-medical-conditions) lists:

| Procedure | Avianca: travel permitted after |
|---|---|
| Breast augmentation, breast reduction, breast lift (with or without implants) | 14 days |
| Liposuction, mini tummy tuck, tummy tuck, lipoabdominoplasty | 14 days |
| Buttock fat injection | 10 days |
| Buttock surgery and implants, gluteoplasty | 14 days |
| Rhinoplasty and/or septoplasty | 10 days |
| Cosmetic facial surgery | 14 days |
| Facelift (rhytidoplasty) or two or more facial procedures combined | 15 days, depending on recovery |
| Two or more body procedures combined (for example, a mommy makeover) | 15 days, depending on recovery |

Planning a nose job? Our guide to [rhinoplasty in Colombia](/blog/rhinoplasty-colombia/) explains how the 10-day rhinoplasty line and the 14-day cosmetic facial surgery line apply, and how to build the stay around them.

Three details in that policy change real itineraries:

1. **The certificate window.** Any special medical condition needs a medical certificate issued no earlier than 10 days before the travel date. A note written on day 3 will not cover a flight on day 15.
2. **Combinations count.** A BBL usually pairs liposuction (listed at 14 days) with fat grafting to the buttocks (listed at 10 days). Plans that combine two or more body procedures fall under the 15-day line. Ask your team which category your certificate will describe.
3. **The airport health authority has the last word.** If your treating doctor and the airport health authority disagree about the days needed, Avianca says the health authority's opinion prevails.

Flying another airline, or connecting through another country? Read that carrier's own medical-travel policy before you buy the ticket. Not every airline publishes procedure-level rules, and the absence of a rule is not a clearance.

## Why online answers range from a few days to six weeks

If you have searched this already, you have probably seen numbers from a few days to more than a month. They are answering different questions:

- **ASPS** (American Society of Plastic Surgeons), in its [briefing paper on cosmetic surgery tourism](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/briefing-papers/briefing-paper-cosmetic-surgery-tourism), suggests waiting five to seven days after body procedures such as liposuction and breast augmentation, and seven to 10 days after facial procedures including facelifts, eyelid surgery, and rhinoplasty, before flying.
- **CDC's Yellow Book** [medical tourism chapter](https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/health-care-abroad/medical-tourism.html) says medical tourists should not fly for 10 days after chest or abdominal surgery, because cabin pressure is roughly equivalent to 6,000 to 8,000 feet of altitude.
- **Avianca's** day counts are a boarding rule for that airline, and they are longer than both.
- **Individual surgeons** often add time for long-haul routes, drains, combined procedures, or personal clot risk.

None of these figures is a personal clearance. They are planning floors. ASPS and CDC both stress that surgery and long flights each raise the risk of blood clots on their own, and that combining them raises it further.

## What usually has to be true before a surgeon signs off

Clearance to fly comes from an in-person check, not from a calendar. These are the questions worth asking your team to answer at the pre-flight visit:

- Is the wound healing as expected, with no sign of infection or fluid collection?
- If drains were used, have they been removed, or is there a written plan for flying with them?
- Can I walk the length of an airport, sit for the flight, and manage a bag without straining the surgical area?
- Is my pain controlled with the medication I will have on the plane?
- Given my history (age, hormones, prior clots, weight, flight length), what clot-prevention plan applies to me?
- Who do I contact if something changes after I land?

**Red flags in a travel plan:** a return flight booked before surgery is even scheduled, a package that assumes you will fly within a few days, or nobody available to examine you before departure.

## Planning the trip around the flight, not the other way round

For U.S. patients, the decision that matters most is made before surgery: how many nights to book in Colombia. Working back from the rules above:

1. Find the longest waiting period that applies to your plan (combined procedures usually set it).
2. Add your arrival days for the in-person evaluation and any tests requested before surgery.
3. Add margin for a later clearance than expected. Keep the return ticket changeable.
4. Schedule the pre-flight visit and the medical certificate inside the 10-day window before departure.

## On the plane: lowering clot risk

The [CDC's guidance on blood clots and travel](https://www.cdc.gov/blood-clots/risk-factors/travel.html) applies to any trip longer than four hours and lists recent surgery (within three months) as a risk factor. Its general measures:

- Move your legs often and exercise your calf muscles; get up and stretch on long flights.
- Ask your doctor whether graduated compression stockings make sense for you.
- If you take blood thinners, follow your doctor's instructions exactly; do not start or stop any medication on your own because of the flight.

Add your surgeon's specific instructions on garments, seating, and walking. An aisle seat makes it easier to stand up regularly.

**Know the warning signs.** CDC lists swelling, unexplained pain or tenderness, warmth, or redness in a leg or arm as possible signs of a deep vein clot, and difficulty breathing, chest pain that worsens with a deep breath, a fast or irregular heartbeat, coughing up blood, or fainting as possible signs of a pulmonary embolism. The second group needs emergency care right away, whether you are still in Cartagena or already home.

## Frequently asked questions

**How soon can I fly after plastic surgery?**
It depends on the procedure, your recovery, and the airline. On Avianca, the published minimum ranges from 10 days (rhinoplasty, buttock fat injection) to 15 days (facelift or combined procedures), with a medical certificate issued within 10 days of the flight. Your surgeon may ask you to wait longer.

**Can I fly 7 days after liposuction?**
ASPS suggests five to seven days after body procedures such as liposuction, but Avianca lists 14 days after liposuction. If you are flying Avianca from Colombia, plan on at least 14 days and your surgeon's clearance.

**How long after a BBL can I fly?**
Avianca lists 10 days after buttock fat injection and 14 days after liposuction. Because a BBL normally includes liposuction to harvest the fat, and plans combining two or more body procedures fall under Avianca's 15-day line, ask your team which category your certificate will describe. Also ask how you should sit during the flight; that guidance is individual.

**How long after a tummy tuck can I fly home?**
Avianca lists 14 days after a tummy tuck, mini tummy tuck, or lipoabdominoplasty, and 15 days when it is combined with other body procedures. CDC advises against flying for 10 days after abdominal surgery.

**Do I need a medical certificate to fly after surgery?**
For Avianca, yes: any special medical condition requires a certificate issued no earlier than 10 days before travel stating that you are fit to fly.

**Is a short flight safer than a long-haul flight?**
CDC notes that clot risk rises with longer periods of immobility, and that flights over four hours carry a recognized risk. A shorter flight does not remove the airline's waiting period.
