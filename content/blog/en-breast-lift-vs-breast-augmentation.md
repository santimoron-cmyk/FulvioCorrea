**A breast lift raises and reshapes. Breast augmentation adds volume.** That is the decision in one line, and it is why “breast lift vs breast augmentation” is not a popularity contest. The American Society of Plastic Surgeons (ASPS) frames the choice around three patient-specific points: where your nipples sit, whether you want more volume, and the overall size you want to reach. You can need a lift alone, implants alone, or both in one operation (augmentation mastopexy). The wrong default is assuming every emptied breast needs an implant, or that an implant will fix a nipple that already points down.

Criteria, limits, combining, recovery, travel, and what to prepare before a Cartagena consult—not a substitute for an exam.

## Decision table: position, volume, size

ASPS and Mayo Clinic give a filter you can start with in a mirror—without calling it a diagnosis.

| What you notice | What it usually points to | Typical starting conversation |
|---|---|---|
| Nipples sit below the crease or point downward when unsupported | Position / ptosis | Breast lift (mastopexy) |
| Breasts look flat or emptied after pregnancy, breastfeeding or weight loss, nipples still high | Lost volume with decent position | Breast augmentation |
| You want a larger cup and nipples already sit well | Size goal with good shape | Breast augmentation |
| Low position **and** you want more upper fullness or a larger size | Position + volume | Lift with implants (augmentation mastopexy) |
| Low position and you want to be smaller or lighter | Position + excess tissue | Lift with reduction |
| One breast sits lower or looks emptier than the other | Asymmetry of position, volume, or both | Individualized plan |

ASPS: nipples pointing down or below the crease push toward a lift, which also repositions the nipple–areola. Volume is separate—pregnancy and breastfeeding can leave breasts looking deflated; an implant can restore roundness, and a lift may still be needed if skin is lax. Size goals live in the augmentation conversation. Mayo Clinic adds similar lift cues: flatter or longer breasts, stretched areolae, or one breast hanging lower.

## What each operation cannot do

**A breast lift will not:**

- significantly change size or round out the upper pole alone. ASPS: if you want fuller breasts, consider lift plus augmentation; if smaller, lift plus reduction;
- avoid scars (around the areola, down to the crease, sometimes along the crease—“around,” “lollipop,” or “anchor”);
- freeze the result forever. Mayo Clinic notes aging and gravity continue; heavier breasts sag again more easily; a future pregnancy can stretch the result;
- replace the implant decision if upper fullness is the real goal.

**Breast augmentation will not:**

- reliably correct sagging that needs a lift. Mayo Clinic: implants do not keep breasts from sagging; a lift may be suggested with augmentation. ASPS: augmentation alone does not fix severe droop;
- last a lifetime without further decisions (Mayo Clinic discusses implant lifespan and imaging follow-up for silicone);
- guarantee a cup size or a social-media look. ISAPS warns implants that are too large can weigh the breast down and work against a lift.

Neither is a weight-loss tool.

## When both is the honest answer

ISAPS describes **augmentation mastopexy** when breasts need to be lifted and reshaped *and* enlarged. Reasonable volume but low position → lift alone may suffice. Small breast with good shape → augmentation alone may suffice. Low-lying *and* small, or very loose skin plus a size goal → combining often contours better than either alone.

ASPS: together or staged. One recovery is convenient; staging is sometimes safer when tissue or implant size make a single session less predictable—the examining surgeon decides. Postpartum multi-area plans lean toward a [mommy makeover](/procedures/mommy-makeover/) calendar: [how long to stay after a mommy makeover](/blog/how-long-to-stay-after-mommy-makeover/).

## Scars, support and under-discussed trade-offs

Lift scars are the price of repositioning skin and nipple. Mayo Clinic: scars soften over about 1–2 years and are usually hidden by bras and swimsuits; thick scars can still occur. Risks include lasting sensory change, asymmetry, trouble making enough milk, and—rarely—nipple/areola loss if blood supply is compromised. Implants add capsular contracture, malposition, rupture, infection and sensory change (Mayo Clinic), plus uncommon implant-associated lymphoma risks for certain textured devices. ISAPS: combined surgery carries both risk sets. Support bras matter: Mayo Clinic describes surgical then soft support after a lift; ISAPS often advises 1–3 months after augmentation mastopexy. Your surgeon’s protocol overrides any blog table.

## Recovery comparison (general sources vs clinic protocol)

Exact clearance is individualized. Below is **general** guidance from ASPS, Mayo Clinic and ISAPS—not your personal calendar.

| | Lift alone | Augmentation alone | Augmentation mastopexy (ISAPS) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early feel | Swelling/bruising often ~2 weeks | Soreness and swelling for a few weeks | Swollen, tight, tender especially first 2 weeks, often up to 6 |
| Light work | Surgeon-directed, often discussed in weeks | Many return within a few weeks if the job is not strenuous (Mayo) | Often 1–2 weeks off |
| Exercise | Gradual; avoid early strain (Mayo) | Avoid straining activity that raises pulse/BP ≥ ~2 weeks (Mayo) | Often 6–8 weeks |
| Support | Surgical then soft support bra for weeks (Mayo) | Compression/support as directed | Commonly 1–3 months |

Cartagena clinic protocol (garment, drains, in-person check before flying, remote follow-up) is set after your exam and may be stricter. Separate society averages from your clearance.

## Travel: Avianca treats lift and implants the same

On Avianca’s plastic-surgery list, **mammoplasty (augmentation or reduction)** and **breast pexy with or without implants** share a **14-day** minimum before travel, plus a medical certificate issued no more than **10 days** before the flight. Those are boarding rules on top of your surgeon’s clearance. If Airport Health disagrees with the day count, Avianca states the health authority prevails.

Lift vs implants vs both does **not** shorten that line; combining breast with another body procedure can. Full table: [flying after plastic surgery](/blog/flying-after-plastic-surgery/). What changes is how those weeks feel—companion help, elevator lodging, front-opening clothes over a support bra, a changeable return ticket. ## Before you book: photos and questions

1. Front, three-quarter and side photos standing relaxed, arms down, no push-up bra.
2. Pregnancy/breastfeeding history and whether more children are planned (Mayo Clinic often suggests waiting about 6–12 months after stopping breastfeeding so size and shape stabilize).
3. Weight stability over the past year.
4. Prior breast surgery, mammograms/biopsies, smoking, medications, clotting problems or diabetes.
5. One clear goal sentence: “higher position,” “more upper fullness,” “one cup larger,” or “smaller and lifted.”

Ask: Is the main issue position, volume, size, or a mix? If implants alone, what happens to nipple position and laxity? If lift alone, will I still miss upper-pole fullness? Same-day combination or staged—and why? Which Avianca line will my certificate describe, and when do you examine me before I fly?

**Red flags:** a surgery date before photos and history, a guaranteed cup, “implants will lift everything,” or a return flight a few days after surgery.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a lift or implants?**
ASPS’s three criteria: nipple position, desire for more volume, and size goals. Nipples below the crease or pointing down → lift conversation; more roundness or a larger size → implants; many people need both.

**Does a breast lift make breasts bigger?**
Not meaningfully. ASPS: a lift does not significantly change size or upper fullness—add augmentation for fullness, or reduction to go smaller.

**Can implants fix sagging?**
Not reliably when true ptosis is present. Mayo Clinic: implants do not keep breasts from sagging; a lift may be recommended with augmentation.

**What is mastopexy vs augmentation?**
Mastopexy = breast lift (raise and reshape). Augmentation = add volume with implants (or, in selected cases, fat). Augmentation mastopexy combines both.

**How long after breastfeeding should I wait?**
Mayo Clinic commonly suggests about six months to a year after you stop breastfeeding.

**How soon can I fly?**
If you fly Avianca, plan around **14 days** after mammoplasty (augmentation or reduction) or breast pexy with or without implants, plus a certificate dated within **10 days** of the flight. Surgeon clearance comes first.

**Breastfeeding later?**
Often still possible, but some people make less milk—ask in consult if future pregnancies matter.
