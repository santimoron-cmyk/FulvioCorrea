**Most people need one to two weeks after breast augmentation before daily life and desk work feel manageable, three to four weeks before lifting or exercise, and about three months before the result starts to look final.** The Aesthetic Society puts typical recovery at one to two weeks and says pain is usually worst in the first 48 hours. University of Utah Health asks patients not to lift, push or pull more than 10 pounds for three to four weeks, and the Colombian Society of Plastic Surgery (SCCP) places the return to sport at around three to four weeks. Final results begin to show at about three months, with subtle changes for up to a year. If you are recovering in Colombia, plan the flight home on the window the American Society of Plastic Surgeons (ASPS) suggests: **5–7 days** after breast augmentation.

Treat these as general ranges. Implant placement, anything done in the same operation, your job and how you heal all move the dates, and the plan that applies to you is the one your surgeon writes after the operation.

## Breast augmentation recovery week by week

The milestones below combine patient guidance from the American Society of Plastic Surgeons (ASPS), The Aesthetic Society, Mayo Clinic, University of Utah Health and the SCCP. Where they give different numbers, both are shown.

**Day of surgery.** Most people go home the same day, usually once they are stable after about an hour in recovery (ASPS, Mayo Clinic). Your chest is covered with gauze and an elastic bandage or support bra. Arrange for someone to take you back to your lodging and stay at least the first night (Mayo Clinic). Walking starts now: The Aesthetic Society recommends a few minutes on your feet every few hours to lower the risk of blood clots.

**Days 1 to 3.** This is the hardest stretch. Expect tightness, swelling, sensitivity to touch and difficulty raising your arms. The Aesthetic Society notes that pain is usually greater when the implants sit under the chest muscle, and ASPS says acute pain typically eases within one to five days. Rest and sleep on your back with your upper body raised; The Aesthetic Society suggests an incline of 25 to 45 degrees, using a wedge pillow or recliner. The SCCP notes that many everyday activities can be picked up again around the third day.

**Days 4 to 7.** Stiffness and soreness usually settle after two to five days. Dressings and drains, if you have them, generally come off within the first week (The Aesthetic Society, University of Utah Health). Showering is allowed anywhere from day one to day seven depending on the surgeon; Utah allows a daily shower from day two. Many people with desk jobs return within a few days to a week, while Mayo Clinic speaks of "within a few weeks" for jobs that do not involve much movement. How physical your work is decides which end of that range applies to you.

**Week 2.** External stitches come out at about one week according to The Aesthetic Society, or at 10 to 14 days according to the SCCP. Mayo Clinic asks patients to avoid anything that strains them or raises their pulse or blood pressure for at least two weeks. The SCCP advises staying out of swimming pools for the first two weeks because of infection risk, and The Aesthetic Society recommends no sexual activity for at least two weeks.

**Weeks 3 to 4.** University of Utah Health keeps the 10-pound limit and the ban on strenuous activity and repetitive arm movements through weeks three and four, and starts upper-body stretching and range-of-motion exercises at about three weeks. Side sleeping usually waits until three to four weeks. Most residual swelling has gone within a month (The Aesthetic Society).

**Weeks 4 to 8.** Exercise comes back gradually, starting from your surgeon's clearance rather than your old routine. The Aesthetic Society asks patients to stay very gentle with their breasts for at least another month after the first few weeks. The SCCP describes the scar as firm and reddish for the first six weeks.

**Months 3 to 12.** Final results start to show at around three months, with subtle changes through the first year (University of Utah Health). Scars fade to thin, slightly discolored lines over about a year but do not disappear completely (University of Utah Health, Mayo Clinic).

## When can you shower, drive, work and sleep on your side?

Most recovery questions are really about one activity. This is what the general sources say about each.

| Activity | What general sources say |
|---|---|
| Walking | From the day of surgery, a few minutes every few hours (The Aesthetic Society, University of Utah Health) |
| Showering | Between day 1 and day 7 depending on the surgeon (The Aesthetic Society); daily from day 2 (University of Utah Health) |
| Bath, pool, hot tub | Not until the incisions have healed or your surgeon allows it (University of Utah Health); no pool for the first two weeks (SCCP) |
| Driving | Only after at least 24 hours off prescription pain medication and once you can handle traffic safely; most people within a week (University of Utah Health) |
| Desk work | Within a few days to a week (The Aesthetic Society, University of Utah Health); within a few weeks if the job does not involve much movement (Mayo Clinic) |
| Sleeping on your side | After three to four weeks, alternating sides at first; on your stomach only when your surgeon says so (University of Utah Health) |
| Lifting, pushing or pulling over 10 lb | Not for three to four weeks (University of Utah Health) |
| Exercise and sport | Nothing that strains you or raises your pulse for at least two weeks (Mayo Clinic); sport again at around three to four weeks (SCCP) |
| Sexual activity | Not for at least two weeks (The Aesthetic Society) |
| Support bra | Worn around the clock as instructed (ASPS); a surgical bra for three to four weeks, then a soft supportive sports bra (University of Utah Health); a sports bra 24 hours a day for one month (SCCP) |
| Flying home | 5–7 days after surgery (ASPS). Airlines can have their own rules; check with your airline before booking. |

## What makes breast implant recovery harder or easier?

**Implant placement.** The implant sits either behind the breast tissue (subglandular) or behind the chest muscle (submuscular), as Mayo Clinic describes; the SCCP also lists a subfascial option, behind the gland and under the thin layer covering the muscle. The Aesthetic Society notes that augmentation stretches the tissue and can be painful, especially with implants under the pectoral muscle. Placement is a surgical decision made at your evaluation, so ask how the choice made for you changes the first week.

**Other surgery in the same operation.** Adding a lift (augmentation mastopexy) means healing the implant pocket and the repositioned skin at the same time. The side-by-side recovery comparison is in [breast lift vs breast augmentation](/blog/breast-lift-vs-breast-augmentation/). If breast surgery is part of a mommy makeover with a tummy tuck or liposuction, the combined calendar is in [mommy makeover recovery time](/blog/mommy-makeover-recovery-time/).

**Smoking.** Mayo Clinic says surgeons ask patients to stop smoking for a time before and after surgery, which may be four to six weeks.

**Your job and your home.** A job that involves lifting can take longer or need temporary restrictions (University of Utah Health). The 10-pound limit matters at home too: laundry baskets, grocery bags and most toddlers weigh more than that, so line up help before surgery, not after.

## What is normal, and when to call your surgeon

Some of what you feel in the first weeks is expected. General sources list:

- tightness, sensitivity, and warm or itchy skin over the breasts (The Aesthetic Society)
- bruising, limited shoulder movement and a small amount of fluid on the gauze (University of Utah Health)
- more or less nipple sensation for a couple of months, and tingling or shooting pains for the first few months (University of Utah Health)
- some temporary asymmetry, because each breast can heal at a different rate (University of Utah Health)

Contact your surgical team promptly if you notice:

- a sudden increase in the size of one side of the chest (SCCP); ASPS notes that bleeding into the implant pocket is possible in the first several days, which is why it asks patients to take it easy until cleared
- pain that does not ease with your prescribed medication, or that is extreme or long-lasting (SCCP, The Aesthetic Society)
- warmth and redness of the breast, fever, or pus at the incisions (Mayo Clinic, SCCP)
- swelling, pain, warmth or redness in a leg (CDC)

Shortness of breath or chest pain needs medical help immediately (Mayo Clinic, CDC), whether you are still in Cartagena or already home.

## Recovering in Cartagena and flying home

### How many days before you can fly

The American Society of Plastic Surgeons (ASPS), in its [briefing paper on cosmetic surgery tourism](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/briefing-papers/briefing-paper-cosmetic-surgery-tourism), suggests waiting **5–7 days** after body procedures such as breast augmentation before flying. That is the window this page uses. Your surgeon's clearance still comes first, and drains, stitches, or another procedure in the same operation can move the date.

Airlines can have their own rules. Avianca, for example, publishes a 14-day minimum after mammoplasty; that is an airline boarding rule, not the recovery window above. Check with your airline before you book. If the same operation includes another body procedure, use that procedure's longer window. The full table by procedure is in [flying after plastic surgery](/blog/flying-after-plastic-surgery/).

### Planning the stay

Build the trip backwards from the 5–7 day window:

1. Your arrival days for the in-person evaluation and any tests requested before surgery.
2. The surgery and the first week, the part you want to spend resting near your follow-up appointments, not in transit.
3. The ASPS window of 5–7 days after breast augmentation. If the plan combines other body procedures, use that procedure's longer window.
4. A pre-flight check with your surgeon, and a medical certificate if your airline asks for one.
5. A margin in case clearance comes later than planned, and a changeable return ticket.

Cartagena makes the second week tempting. The CDC and ASPS both warn that typical vacation activities after surgery, including sunbathing, swimming, alcohol, strenuous exercise and long walking or bus tours, can delay healing. The beaches and the walled city will still be there once you are cleared. For how a visit for [breast augmentation in Cartagena](/procedures/breast-augmentation/) is organized, and what the clinic's [international patients](/international-patients/) program covers before and after the trip, see those pages.

### On the plane

A long flight soon after surgery adds its own risk. The CDC lists recent surgery, within three months, as a risk factor for travel-related blood clots on trips over four hours, and recommends moving your legs often, flexing your ankles and asking your doctor whether graduated compression stockings make sense for you. An aisle seat makes getting up easier. Because the 10-pound limit usually still applies, plan for someone else to lift your bag into the overhead bin, and travel in a loose shirt that buttons or zips at the front so you do not have to raise your arms (University of Utah Health suggests the same kind of clothing for the first weeks).

### What to bring home

Before you leave Colombia, ask for your implant records. Mayo Clinic advises reading and keeping copies of the patient information from your implant's manufacturer, and the FDA's labeling recommendations for breast implants include a patient device card. The CDC Yellow Book tells medical tourists to request copies of their medical records in English and share them with their doctors at home. Those records matter later, too: the FDA suggests imaging five to six years after silicone implants are placed and every two to three years after that (as summarized by Mayo Clinic), and the SCCP recommends that mammograms be read at a center experienced with implants.

## Questions to settle before you book

1. Will my implants go over or under the muscle, and how does that change the first week?
2. Will I have drains, and when do the drains and stitches usually come out?
3. Which bra or band will I wear, for how long, and when can I switch to a regular sports bra?
4. When is my in-person check before the flight, and who issues the airline certificate?
5. What happens if my clearance comes later than my ticket?
6. How does follow-up work once I am home, and who do I contact after hours while I am still in Cartagena?
7. Which implant documents will I take home?

## Frequently asked questions

**How long is breast augmentation recovery?**
The Aesthetic Society says recovery typically takes one to two weeks. Lifting and exercise usually wait three to four weeks (University of Utah Health, SCCP), and the final result starts to show at around three months.

**How painful is recovery from breast implants?**
Pain is usually worst in the first 48 hours and tends to be greater with implants under the muscle (The Aesthetic Society). ASPS says acute pain typically eases within one to five days, though soreness and swelling can last a few weeks.

**When can I go back to work after breast augmentation?**
For desk work, often within a few days to a week (The Aesthetic Society, University of Utah Health). Mayo Clinic speaks of a few weeks. Jobs with lifting take longer.

**How long do I wear a surgical bra after breast augmentation?**
It depends on the surgeon. University of Utah Health describes a surgical bra for three to four weeks followed by a soft sports bra, and the SCCP describes a sports bra worn 24 hours a day for one month.

**How long should I stay in Colombia after breast augmentation?**
ASPS suggests 5–7 days after breast augmentation before flying, plus your arrival days and a margin. If another body procedure is done at the same time, that procedure's longer window applies. The final date depends on your surgeon's clearance. Airlines can have their own rules, so check with your airline before booking.
