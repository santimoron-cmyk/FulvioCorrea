**Most people are back at a desk within a few days to two weeks after liposuction, wear a compression garment for about four to six weeks, avoid strenuous exercise for four to six weeks, and see the final shape only after several months.** The Aesthetic Society says swelling peaks around 48 hours after surgery, is mostly gone within two to three weeks, and can linger slightly for up to four months. The American Society of Plastic Surgeons (ASPS) expects bruising to be gone by weeks four to five and gentle exercise to start from week six. If you have surgery in Colombia, the date that usually decides your trip is the flight home: ASPS suggests waiting **5–7 days** after liposuction, but Avianca, Colombia's flag carrier, lists **14 days**.

These are general ranges, not a personal plan. How many areas are treated, how much fat is removed, whether anything else is done in the same operation and how your body heals all move the dates. Your surgeon sets your timeline after surgery.

## Liposuction recovery time: the short answer

- **Back to a desk job:** within a few days for many people (Mayo Clinic, The Aesthetic Society); ASPS places the return to work at weeks two to three, depending on the job.
- **Compression garment:** usually four to six weeks (The Aesthetic Society); Mayo Clinic says "a few weeks." Your surgeon sets the type and duration.
- **Exercise:** strenuous exercise waits four to six weeks (The Aesthetic Society) and ASPS starts gentle exercise from week six. The NHS is more cautious: gentle exercise at three to four weeks and nothing strenuous for 10 to 12 weeks.
- **Sun and heat:** none for at least six weeks, including sunbathing (The Aesthetic Society).
- **Flying home from Colombia:** at least five to seven days after surgery as a recovery minimum (ASPS); Avianca lists 14 days, or 15 when two or more body procedures are combined, with a medical certificate.
- **Final result:** the area looks slimmer within several months (Mayo Clinic) and can take up to six months to settle (NHS).

## Liposuction recovery day by day and week by week

The milestones below come from patient guidance published by ASPS, The Aesthetic Society, Mayo Clinic, the NHS and the Colombian Society of Plastic Surgery (SCCP). Where they disagree, both numbers are shown, because the disagreement itself tells you how much recovery varies.

**Surgery day.** You wake up in a compression garment or elastic bandages, sometimes with small drains. Mayo Clinic says most people spend at least a few hours being monitored, and an overnight stay is possible if a lot of fluid was removed. Whatever happens, someone needs to take you back to your lodging and stay with you at least the first night (Mayo Clinic); the NHS asks for 24 hours of company after general anesthesia.

**Days 1 to 3.** This is the uncomfortable stretch. The Aesthetic Society describes the feeling as soreness more than sharp pain, with swelling that peaks around 48 hours and incisions that may leak fluid for several days. Rest, but do not stay in bed: the SCCP recommends walking with someone from the first day after surgery, taking short walks often and breathing deeply, and flexing and stretching your arms and legs while resting to help circulation. Avoid bending, straining and lifting in this early period (The Aesthetic Society).

**Days 4 to 7.** Bruising starts to fade; The Aesthetic Society says it typically disappears within seven to ten days, while ASPS places it fully gone by weeks four to five. Expect both, depending on how many areas were treated. Stitches, if you have them, usually come out at seven to ten days (The Aesthetic Society) or after about a week (NHS). Many people with desk jobs can work again within a few days (Mayo Clinic, The Aesthetic Society). The NHS allows the garment to come off while you shower.

**Weeks 2 to 3.** ASPS describes this as the point where you start to feel much better and may return to work, depending on the job, while still limiting exercise. The SCCP places the gradual return to daily activities around the first to second week. The Aesthetic Society says most people resume most normal activities within ten days or less. Most of the swelling has gone by the end of week three.

**Weeks 4 to 6.** The compression garment usually stays on for four to six weeks (The Aesthetic Society), and strenuous exercise waits for the same four to six weeks because it can bring fluid back into the treated areas. The NHS is more cautious: gentle exercise at around three to four weeks and nothing strenuous for 10 to 12 weeks, always after checking with your surgeon. Some areas may feel firm or slightly uneven at this stage.

**Months 2 to 6.** Mayo Clinic says swelling typically goes away within a few weeks and the treated area looks slimmer within several months. The Aesthetic Society warns that a fluctuating 10 to 15 percent of swelling can return with exercise or a very active day for six to nine months, which is why judging the result at week four is premature. Numbness usually fades within six to eight weeks (NHS).

## What makes liposuction recovery longer or shorter?

**How much is treated.** One small area and a full midsection are different recoveries. Mayo Clinic says the risk of complications rises when the surgeon works on larger body surfaces or does several procedures in the same operation, and large-volume liposuction can shift enough fluid to affect the kidneys, heart and lungs. Circumferential liposuction of the abdomen, waist and back, often marketed as "lipo 360," sits at the larger end of that range. What a lipo 360 costs, and which services a quote should list, is in [lipo 360 in Colombia: cost and what the price includes](/blog/lipo-360-colombia-cost/).

**What else is done in the same operation.** A BBL adds fat grafting to the buttocks, with its own sitting and safety rules; those are covered in [BBL Colombia: recovery time and how long to stay](/blog/bbl-colombia/). If loose skin or the abdominal wall is part of the problem, the comparison in [tummy tuck vs liposuction](/blog/tummy-tuck-vs-liposuction/) explains why the recovery is very different.

**Your job.** A desk job can restart within days. A job with lifting, long hours standing or heat exposure takes longer.

**Smoking and medication.** The Aesthetic Society asks patients not to smoke, to avoid alcohol while on pain medication and to avoid aspirin and certain anti-inflammatory drugs; Mayo Clinic says surgeons usually ask you to stop blood thinners and NSAIDs at least a week before surgery. Never stop a prescribed medication without asking the doctor who prescribed it.

## Compression garment and massage: where sources agree and where they don't

Every source agrees that you will wear a compression garment. They disagree on how long:

- **Mayo Clinic:** "a few weeks."
- **The Aesthetic Society:** four to six weeks over the treated areas, to control swelling and help the skin contract.
- **NHS:** constantly for several weeks.
- **One surgeon interviewed by ASPS:** four to eight weeks with a customized garment.

The SCCP adds a detail the U.S. pages skip: firm areas (fibrosis) are frequent after liposuction and often need specialized massage, and loose areas may need extra localized pressure inside the garment.

Lymphatic drainage massage is where online advice diverges most. An ASPS article notes that every surgeon has their own rules and that many have liposuction patients start lymphatic massage the day after surgery and continue several times a week for two months; other practices start later or not at all. There is no universal schedule. Ask your surgeon three things: whether you need massage at all, when it starts, and who performs it. A trained therapist working on fresh tissue is not a spa appointment.

## Recovery by treated area

The week-by-week timeline above is the general course. It is not the same clock for every part of the body. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/11009-liposuction) says each person's recovery differs with how much fat is removed and from which area, and that healing can take up to six months. For part of that time, usually the first six weeks, the advice is to slow down and do fewer activities. The [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/cosmetic-procedures/cosmetic-surgery/liposuction/) says the return to work depends on the job and on how much of the body was treated. These are published ranges. They vary from person to person. The plan Dr. Fulvio Correa gives you after your evaluation is the one that applies.

A 2013 review in the *Indian Journal of Plastic Surgery* describes swelling after liposuction as expected. It appears within 24 to 48 hours, can keep rising mildly for 10 to 14 days, and is usually managed with a compression garment for about four to six weeks. The area tends to feel firmer by two to three weeks, soften through six to eight weeks, and feel pliant again around three months when a larger area was treated. A 2023 review in *Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery Global Open* found limited high-quality evidence that compression garments change results after body contouring, and it notes downsides of a poor fit, including discomfort, venous stasis (blood pooling in the veins) and skin problems. Patient guides from ASPS, Cleveland Clinic, Mayo Clinic and the NHS still describe a garment as part of recovery. The type, the fit and the hours come from your surgeon.

That 2013 review also warns that a garment that fits badly, especially with poor posture, can leave dents or grooves. A garment that hurts, or that makes breathing difficult, is a reason to call the office. [ASPS](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/the-importance-of-compression-after-plastic-surgery) says the garment should fit snugly and should not be so tight that it is uncomfortable or restricts breathing. The 2013 review, citing earlier literature, says that getting moving early after surgery lowers the risk of deep-vein thrombosis.

### Abdomen and flanks

Cleveland Clinic lists the abdomen, waist and flanks among the most common areas. Bruising and swelling follow the general timeline higher on this page. On the [ASPS recovery table](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/liposuction/recovery), bruising should be gone by weeks four to five and swelling starts to subside then. The 2013 review adds the tissue feel: soft swelling that can still rise for 10 to 14 days, a firmer feel by the end of two to three weeks, and softening through six to eight weeks. Because this is often a large surface, the amount removed matters. Mayo Clinic notes that the risk of complications rises when surgery covers a larger body surface or more than one procedure.

**Garment.** A compression garment over the treated torso. ASPS describes an elastic bandage or a compression garment after liposuction, and some lower-body garments have an opening so you can use the bathroom without taking the garment off. Wear time in the patient guides runs from a few weeks (Mayo Clinic) to several weeks (Cleveland Clinic and the NHS) to about four to six weeks in the 2013 review. If the skin of a large abdomen is loose, that review says the garment may stay on longer than the usual six weeks, sometimes eight to 12 weeks, so the skin has more time to retract. Your surgeon decides which garment and how long.

**Sitting, walking and mobility.** The NHS says walking and ordinary movement should be fine, and that anything more strenuous waits through the first couple of weeks. ASPS asks for activity to be significantly limited in week one, and for exercise to stay limited in weeks two and three. These pages do not publish a rule against sitting after abdominal liposuction. Pain that is sharp when you sit, stand or walk is a reason to call your surgeon.

**Return to usual activity.** Mayo Clinic says many people wait a few days before work and a few weeks before usual activities, including exercise. ASPS places the return to work at weeks two to three, depending on the job, and gentle exercise from week six. The NHS is more cautious about effort: gentle exercise around three to four weeks, and nothing strenuous for 10 to 12 weeks. A job with lifting, bending or long hours on your feet takes longer than a desk, because the NHS ties the date to the work itself.

The 2013 review states that fluid pockets (seromas) are most common in the lower abdomen and in the outer and posterior thighs. Tell your surgeon about a soft swelling that grows.

### Arms

Cleveland Clinic lists the upper arms as a common site. Bruising, swelling and soreness are the healing signs it describes for liposuction in general, and they fade as the body heals. ASPS, Mayo Clinic, Cleveland Clinic and the NHS do not publish a separate week-by-week calendar for the arms alone, so the general ranges are the ones in print. The 2013 review flags bulky arms with loose skin as an area where leftover looseness is more likely, and where the garment may be worn past six weeks, sometimes for eight to 12 weeks. That is not the same operation as an arm lift. The 2023 review found little high-quality evidence on compression after limb-contouring surgery, including arm lifts, so the arm garment is one your surgeon specifies. Cleveland Clinic says the garment goes on the treated area for several weeks.

**Movement.** These sources do not add a walking limit that applies only to the arms. The limits they do publish are the general ones: much less activity in week one (ASPS), ordinary movement without strenuous effort for the first couple of weeks (NHS), and fewer activities for about the first six weeks (Cleveland Clinic). Reaching, lifting and pushing use the treated area, so work that depends on the arms follows the NHS point that the return to work depends on the job.

**Return to usual activity.** The same published windows as the abdomen. Mayo Clinic's general liposuction guidance is a few days before work and a few weeks before usual activity, including exercise. The ASPS table places work at weeks two to three, depending on the job, and gentle exercise from week six. The NHS asks you to wait 10 to 12 weeks before strenuous use of the arms.

### Thighs

Cleveland Clinic lists the thighs, and also the inner knee, calves and ankles. The NHS specifically names swollen ankles when the legs or ankles are treated. Bruising and swelling can last up to six months (NHS). The swelling course in the 2013 review still applies, and that review states that seromas are most common in the outer and posterior thighs and the lower abdomen. Inner thighs with loose skin are one of the areas where it says the garment may be needed for longer than six weeks.

**Garment.** The NHS describes an elasticated support corset or compression bandages, worn constantly for several weeks and taken off to shower. ASPS says there are compression garments for nearly every part of the body. The 2023 review notes that some published descriptions of calf and ankle liposuction used a surgical compression hose, and that comparative studies have not shown whether compression changes results after body contouring. For the thighs, the garment that covers the treated legs is the one your surgeon fits. It should not be tight enough to hurt.

**Walking.** The NHS says you should be able to walk and that general movement should be fine, while avoiding anything more strenuous for the first couple of weeks. The 2013 review says early walking lowers the risk of blood clots. Ankle swelling after the legs are treated is described as common (NHS). A leg that is swollen, painful, warm or red is a different finding. Contact your surgical team.

**Return to usual activity.** The same windows, and they depend on the job. Walking is the early movement these sources describe. Strenuous leg exercise waits on the NHS guide (nothing strenuous for 10 to 12 weeks) or the ASPS table (gentle exercise from week six). Your surgeon clears the date.

### Chin and neck

Cleveland Clinic lists the chin and neck among areas that can be treated with liposuction. [ASPS, describing neck liposuction](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/sculpting-the-male-jawline-chin-and-neck-contouring-101), says to expect bruising and swelling for several days to weeks, and that a compression garment can limit swelling and support healing. The garment [ASPS describes for neck liposuction](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/the-importance-of-compression-after-plastic-surgery) wraps around the head and under the chin. [Cleveland Clinic's guide to double chin surgery](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/how-to-get-rid-of-a-double-chin), which includes chin liposuction, says you can usually go home the same day, with bandages and possibly a chin strap, and that pain, bruising and swelling should improve within about two weeks. Incisions can take several months to heal. If drains are used, that guide says they are removed a few days later, at the first follow-up.

**Garment.** A chin strap, or a wrap that goes under the chin and around the head, not the torso garment used on the abdomen. The ASPS compression article describes day-and-night wear, except to bathe, for about one to three weeks, then at night for several more weeks once the surgeon agrees. It says the exact time varies with the person and the procedure. That window is shorter than the four-to-six-week body garment in the 2013 review, which is why the chin and neck are listed apart. It is still a published range. Your surgeon sets the hours.

**Sitting, walking and mobility.** These liposuction pages do not restrict sitting or walking after chin and neck liposuction, and they do not say to keep the neck still. ASPS describes limited neck movement at the start of recovery from a neck lift. A neck lift is a different operation: it treats skin and often muscle, not fat removal alone. Do not apply neck-lift limits to liposuction unless your surgeon gives them to you.

**Return to usual activity.** No ASPS, Mayo Clinic, Cleveland Clinic or NHS page gives a chin-only day for going back to work. The general liposuction figures still apply: Mayo Clinic, a few days before work and a few weeks before usual activity; ASPS, weeks two to three depending on the job. What is specific here is bruising you can see. ASPS describes it for several days to weeks. Cleveland Clinic's double-chin guide expects pain, bruising and swelling to improve within about two weeks, while the incision can take several months.

## What is normal, and when to call your surgeon

Expected in the first weeks, according to general sources:

- swelling and bruising, which can last up to six months in some people (NHS)
- fluid leaking from the small incisions for several days (The Aesthetic Society)
- numbness or tingling in the treated area (NHS, Mayo Clinic)
- firm or slightly uneven areas while swelling settles (SCCP)
- swollen ankles if the legs were treated (NHS)

Contact your surgical team promptly if you notice:

- pain that is extreme or long-lasting, or does not ease with your medication (The Aesthetic Society)
- a soft, growing swelling that could be a fluid pocket (seroma); Mayo Clinic notes these sometimes need to be drained with a needle
- fever, spreading redness or pus at an incision
- swelling, pain, warmth or redness in one leg (CDC)

Shortness of breath or chest pain need emergency care right away (CDC), whether you are still in Cartagena or already home.

## Recovering in Cartagena and flying home

### How many days after liposuction can you fly?

Two published numbers apply, and they are not the same:

- **ASPS**, in its [briefing paper on cosmetic surgery tourism](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/briefing-papers/briefing-paper-cosmetic-surgery-tourism), suggests waiting **five to seven days** after body procedures such as liposuction before flying. This is the figure on our [liposuction procedure page](/procedures/liposuction/), and it describes recovery.
- **Avianca** lists **14 days** after liposuction, and **15 days** when two or more body procedures are combined, in its [help center](https://ayuda.avianca.com/hc/en-us/articles/13090597036315-Are-there-any-travel-restrictions-for-passengers-with-special-medical-conditions). It also requires a medical certificate issued no more than 10 days before the flight. That is a boarding rule for that airline.

So the practical answer depends on how you fly. If you are flying Avianca out of Colombia, plan on at least 14 days, or 15 for a combined plan, plus your surgeon's clearance. On another airline, the ASPS window is the recovery minimum, but check that airline's own rules before you buy the ticket, and keep the return date changeable. The full table by procedure is in [flying after plastic surgery](/blog/flying-after-plastic-surgery/).

### How long should you stay in Colombia after liposuction?

Plan the trip around the flight home, not the surgery date. Count the arrival days for your in-person evaluation, the surgery itself, and then at least five to seven days after surgery (the ASPS recovery minimum) or 14 days if you fly Avianca (15 for combined body procedures). Add the pre-flight check with your surgeon and a few days of margin. The stay plan for [liposuction in Cartagena, Colombia](/procedures/liposuction/) is confirmed at that check, not before surgery.

### Building the stay

1. Arrival days for the in-person evaluation and any tests your surgeon asks for.
2. Surgery and the first week, spent close to your follow-up appointments.
3. The flight window: 5–7 days from ASPS as a recovery minimum, or the airline's rule if it is longer.
4. A pre-flight check with your surgeon and the medical certificate if your airline requires one.
5. A margin of a few days in case clearance comes later than planned.

Where you spend those days matters. Some patients choose a recovery house with nursing support and others choose a hotel or apartment with a companion; the trade-offs are in our guide to [recovery houses in Cartagena](/recovery-house-cartagena/). If you are still planning the budget, [book a valuation](/book-consultation/) and the written quote is what applies to your case. The consultation fee is USD 150 / COP 400,000.

### Heat, sun and the city

Cartagena's heat makes a compression garment harder to tolerate, so pack breathable layers to wear under it and choose air-conditioned lodging. The Aesthetic Society recommends no sun or heat exposure, including sunbathing, for at least six weeks because it can bring swelling back. ASPS and the CDC Yellow Book both warn that vacation activities after surgery, such as sunbathing, swimming, alcohol and long walking or bus tours, can compromise healing. Short walks in the shade, early or late in the day, are part of recovery. A beach day is not, yet.

### On the plane

The CDC lists recent surgery as a risk factor for blood clots on trips longer than four hours and recommends moving your legs often and flexing your ankles. Ask whether you should fly in your garment and whether compression stockings make sense for you. Book an aisle seat, drink water and let someone else lift your bag. Before you leave, ask for copies of your medical records in English, as the CDC Yellow Book recommends for medical tourists.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long is liposuction recovery?**
Most people return to light daily activity and desk work within a few days to two weeks (Mayo Clinic, ASPS, The Aesthetic Society). Strenuous exercise usually waits four to six weeks, and the final result takes several months to show as swelling settles.

**How long do you wear a compression garment after liposuction?**
It depends on the surgeon. The Aesthetic Society says four to six weeks, Mayo Clinic says a few weeks, and the NHS says constantly for several weeks. Your surgeon decides the type and duration.

**When can I go back to work after lipo?**
Often within a few days for a desk job (Mayo Clinic, The Aesthetic Society). ASPS places the return to work at weeks two to three depending on the job. Physical work takes longer.

**How long after liposuction can I fly?**
ASPS suggests five to seven days after body procedures such as liposuction. Avianca lists 14 days after liposuction and 15 if it is combined with another body procedure, with a medical certificate issued within 10 days of the flight. Your surgeon's clearance comes first either way.

**When will I see the final result of liposuction?**
Mayo Clinic says the area looks less bulky once swelling goes down in a few weeks and slimmer within several months. The NHS says it can take up to six months to settle completely.

**Is it normal to feel hard lumps after liposuction?**
Firm areas are common while swelling resolves, and the SCCP notes that fibrosis often needs specialized massage. Tell your surgeon about any lump that grows, hurts more or feels fluid-filled.

**How long should I stay in Colombia after liposuction?**
At least until your surgeon clears you to fly: ASPS suggests five to seven days after liposuction as a recovery minimum, and Avianca requires 14 days (15 for combined body procedures). Add arrival days for the evaluation before surgery and a few days of margin.

**When can I exercise after liposuction?**
The Aesthetic Society says strenuous exercise waits four to six weeks, and ASPS starts gentle exercise from week six. The NHS is more cautious, with gentle exercise at three to four weeks and nothing strenuous for 10 to 12 weeks. Your surgeon clears you.

**Can I go to the beach after liposuction in Cartagena?**
Not in the first weeks. The Aesthetic Society recommends no sun or heat exposure for at least six weeks, and ASPS and the CDC Yellow Book warn that sunbathing and swimming after surgery can compromise healing. Short walks in the shade are fine.

**Does liposuction recovery differ by the area treated?**
Cleveland Clinic says it does: recovery changes with how much fat is removed and from which area. The abdomen, flanks, arms, thighs and chin or neck share the same broad stages, and the garment and the dates are set for your operation. A published range is not your plan.

**What do you wear after chin or neck liposuction?**
ASPS describes a compression garment that wraps around the head and under the chin. Cleveland Clinic's double-chin guide, which includes chin liposuction, says you may go home with bandages and possibly a chin strap. An ASPS article describes day-and-night wear for about one to three weeks, then nights, and says the exact time varies by procedure. Your surgeon sets it.
