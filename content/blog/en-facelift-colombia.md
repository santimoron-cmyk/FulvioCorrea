A **facelift in Colombia** needs a longer trip than most people expect. Avianca, Colombia's flag carrier, permits travel **15 days** after a facelift (rhytidoplasty), depending on recovery, and asks for a medical certificate issued no more than 10 days before the flight. That is longer than the 7 to 10 days the American Society of Plastic Surgeons (ASPS) suggests waiting before flying after facial procedures. Add a day or two before surgery for the in-person evaluation, and a realistic plan is about two and a half weeks in Cartagena, with the final date set by the surgeon who examines you.

The good news is that those weeks are not spent in bed. The Colombian Society of Plastic Surgery (SCCP) says patients are usually up and walking the day after surgery, that most of the bruising has faded by about two weeks, and that people tend to return to everyday activities between days 10 and 15. The trip is long because the face needs time to settle, and because the flight home is the last step, not the first.

## Why a facelift trip runs longer than "fly after a week"

You will find very different numbers online. They answer different questions, so it helps to put them side by side:

| What you are planning around | What the source says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Drains and compressive bandage | Removed the day after surgery | SCCP |
| Stitches | Removed between days 8 and 14 (eyelid stitches, if combined, around days 3 to 5) | SCCP |
| Makeup | Allowed again after the first week | SCCP |
| Bruising and swelling | About 2 to 3 weeks; feeling "back to normal" can take 2 to 3 months | Cleveland Clinic |
| Back to everyday activities | Usually between days 10 and 15 | SCCP |
| Waiting before a flight | 7 to 10 days after facial procedures, facelifts included | ASPS |
| Avianca boarding rule | 15 days after a facelift or two or more combined facial procedures, depending on recovery | Avianca |
| Medical certificate | Issued no more than 10 days before the flight | Avianca |

So the practical rule for a facelift is the latest of three dates: the airline's line, the day you would be comfortable being seen in an airport, and the day your surgeon examines you and signs off. If you add eyelid surgery, a brow lift or a rhinoplasty to the same operation, the 15-day line for combined facial procedures is the one that will usually apply. Our guide to [flying after plastic surgery](/blog/flying-after-plastic-surgery/) has the full airline table and the clot precautions for long flights.

Two booking habits save trouble. Count the days from the surgery date, not from the day you land. And keep the return ticket changeable: Avianca says that if the airport health authority disagrees with your doctor about the days needed, the health authority prevails.

## Deep plane, SMAS, mini: what the names actually mean

Many people researching a **face lift in Colombia** arrive with a technique name already in mind, usually "deep plane." The names describe how deep the surgeon works and which layers are moved, not a guaranteed result.

- **SMAS facelift.** The SMAS is the layer of connective tissue and muscle under the skin. Cleveland Clinic describes the SMAS lift as tightening that layer and trimming excess skin or fat in the cheeks and lower face.
- **Deep plane facelift.** The surgeon lifts the SMAS, fat and skin together as one unit, and it generally treats several areas of the face at once. Cleveland Clinic groups the deep plane, high SMAS and extended SMAS as variations of the same idea: the tension sits on the deeper layer rather than on the skin, which is what gives a longer-lasting result.
- **Mini facelift.** Shorter incisions, usually for people with early sagging of the lower face and neck. Cleveland Clinic notes it typically needs five to seven days off work, against two to three weeks for a more extensive facelift.
- **Skin-only lift.** Tightens the skin alone. Most modern facelifts go deeper than that.

What the evidence does not show is a single winner. A review in *The Laryngoscope* found no established consensus that deep plane is superior to SMAS plication, with a possible advantage of the deep plane in the midface. A systematic review and meta-analysis in *Annals of Plastic Surgery* (47 studies, 10,766 patients) reported similar safety for both, with hematoma rates of 3% for deep techniques and 2% for SMAS, and concluded that the technique should be chosen for each patient.

Dr. Fulvio Correa explains the principle in his facelift video: "A face lift goes far beyond lifting the skin. When we perform this procedure, we also mobilize the layer of tissue that covers, invests, and connects the muscles of the face and neck, also known as the SMAS layer." Which variation fits your face, and whether volume needs to be added where it has been lost, is decided at the consultation.

Better questions than "do you do deep plane?":

1. Which areas of my face and neck will this plan treat, and which will it not?
2. Where will the incisions run, and where will the scars sit relative to my hairline and ears?
3. Do I need a neck lift, eyelid surgery or volume added, or is that optional?
4. Which Avianca line will my medical certificate describe if we combine procedures?
5. How long do you expect visible bruising to last in my case, and when will you examine me before I fly?

## Your first two weeks in Cartagena, one stage at a time

**Surgery day and the first night.** The SCCP says you wake up with drainage tubes and a compressive bandage around the head to prevent hematoma, and that pain after this operation is usually minimal. Mayo Clinic notes that hematoma is the most common complication and usually appears within 24 hours. Cleveland Clinic advises having someone stay with you for at least the first 48 hours. Bring a companion, or ask the team in advance how the first night is covered.

**Days 1 to 7.** The drains and the bandage come off the day after surgery. You can get up, walk, shower and wash your hair. Sleep and rest with the head of the bed raised about 30 degrees to help the swelling drain. Expect bruising during the first week; it turns yellow as it fades.

**Days 8 to 14.** The remaining stitches come out. Makeup is allowed again after the first week. Most bruising has faded by about two weeks, which is also when Avianca's clock is getting close to its 15-day line.

**The weeks after you fly home.** For one month the SCCP advises avoiding strenuous exercise (tennis, jogging, cycling), hair dye, smoking, pools and saunas. Numbness and tightness are normal at first; sensation usually returns within a month, but some people take up to six months.

### Cartagena's sun is part of the plan

Cartagena is bright all year, and sun protection is one of the longest-running instructions after a facelift. The SCCP recommends avoiding direct sun for **at least three months** and using sunscreen continuously for **six months**. Choose lodging with shade and air conditioning, pack a wide-brimmed hat and button-front shirts, and leave the beach, boat trips and long walking tours for another visit. ASPS also tells cosmetic-surgery travelers not to sunbathe, drink alcohol or swim after surgery.

### Signs that need a call to the team right away

The SCCP lists these warning signs after a facelift: increasing swelling of the face, pain that does not improve with medication, changes in skin color or blisters, persistent blurred vision, uncontrollable vomiting, or trouble breathing. Cleveland Clinic adds bleeding that soaks the bandage, fever and pus from an incision. Save the team's emergency number before surgery, not after.

## What to sort out before you book

A facelift plan starts with your health history, and several items take weeks to fix:

- **Smoking.** The SCCP says smokers must stop completely at least two weeks before surgery, because smoking raises the risk of skin loss. Mayo Clinic also links it to poor healing and hematoma.
- **Medicines and supplements that increase bleeding.** The SCCP lists aspirin, anti-inflammatories, vitamin E, omega-3, ginkgo biloba and other herbal products. Stopping anything is done only under medical guidance.
- **Blood pressure.** High blood pressure does not rule you out if it is controlled, according to the SCCP. It matters because uncontrolled pressure raises bleeding risk.
- **Expectations.** A facelift does not remove fine lines or sun damage, and it does not stop aging. Cleveland Clinic says results usually last around 7 to 10 years.

If you live abroad, the first conversation can be virtual. Send clear photos of your face from the front, both profiles and at an angle, mention any earlier facial surgery or fillers, and list your medications. The surgical plan is confirmed in person, with a physical examination, before surgery.

## How to check the surgeon and the facility in Colombia

In his video, Dr. Correa points to the complexity of facial tissue and the large number of nerves in the face as the reason training matters. Who operates matters more than the city. Before you travel you can verify:

1. **ReTHUS.** In May 2026 the SCCP urged patients to confirm that the surgeon has formal training in the procedure and is listed in ReTHUS, Colombia's national registry of health professionals.
2. **Society membership, if claimed.** SCCP membership can be checked in the society's [surgeon search](https://cirugiaplastica.org.co/buscar-cirujano/).
3. **The operating facility.** The same recommendations say surgery belongs in a clinic authorized (*habilitada*) by the health authorities, never in a house or apartment. Ask for its name and address in writing.
4. **Anesthesia.** Ask who provides it and which type is planned. A facelift can be done under local anesthesia with sedation or under general anesthesia, and the SCCP puts the operation at three to seven hours depending on the areas treated.

**Red flags:** a surgery date offered before anyone has seen your photos and history, a return flight booked for a week after surgery, no in-person check before you fly, or any promise of a specific number of years taken off your face.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I need to stay in Colombia after a facelift?**
Plan around Avianca's 15-day line for a facelift, plus your arrival days for the in-person evaluation, which comes to roughly two and a half weeks. ASPS suggests 7 to 10 days before flying after facial procedures, but the airline minimum is longer, and your surgeon's clearance comes first.

**When will I look presentable after a facelift?**
Cleveland Clinic says bruising and swelling last about two to three weeks. The SCCP notes makeup is allowed after the first week and most people return to everyday activities between days 10 and 15. Feeling completely back to normal can take two to three months.

**Is a deep plane facelift better than a SMAS facelift?**
There is no established consensus. Both move the deeper layer of the face rather than the skin alone, published reviews report similar safety, and the deep plane may give more improvement in the midface. The right choice depends on your anatomy and is made at the consultation.

**Can I go to the beach in Cartagena after a facelift?**
Not during recovery. The SCCP advises avoiding direct sun for at least three months, sunscreen for six months, and no pools or saunas for a month. Shade, a hat and indoor plans are part of the trip.

**How long does a facelift last?**
Cleveland Clinic says results usually last around 7 to 10 years. Aging continues, and a facelift does not treat fine lines or sun damage.

**Do I need someone with me?**
Yes, at least for the first day or two. Hematoma, the most common complication, usually appears within 24 hours, and Cleveland Clinic recommends having someone stay with you for at least the first 48 hours.
