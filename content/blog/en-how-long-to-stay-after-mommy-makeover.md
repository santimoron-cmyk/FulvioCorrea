One of the first planning questions U.S. patients ask is **how long to stay after a mommy makeover**—especially when surgery is abroad. The answer depends on which procedures are combined, how recovery is progressing, flight length, and when your surgical team clears you to travel. This guide explains how to think about stay length, what usually shapes the timeline, and what to confirm before you buy a return ticket.

## Why stay length matters more than the surgery day alone

A mommy makeover typically combines procedures that address changes after pregnancy—commonly some mix of tummy tuck, breast surgery, and liposuction. Each component adds swelling, mobility limits, and follow-up needs. Flying home too early can raise discomfort and, in some cases, medical risk. Building a realistic stay into your plan is part of safe medical travel, not an optional extra.

Treat Cartagena (or any destination) as a recovery base for the first phase—not as a vacation bolted onto surgery week. Quiet lodging, help with daily tasks, and easy access to post-op checks usually matter more than sightseeing.

## What “how long to stay” usually means in practice

Patient-education guidance from major societies is deliberately cautious rather than calendar-rigid. The International Society of Aesthetic Plastic Surgery (ISAPS) advises that patients having surgery abroad should stay in the area where the procedure was performed for **at least one week, depending on the procedure**, because traveling too soon can increase the risk of blood clots. An American Society of Plastic Surgeons (ASPS) patient FAQ on mommy makeover recovery notes that **initial recovery typically lasts about two to three weeks**, with a gradual return to normal activities over roughly six to eight weeks—and that full recovery varies with which procedures were combined.

In practical medical-travel planning, those references often translate into a multi-week in-country window when a tummy tuck, drains, or a long-haul flight are involved. Treat any published range as a planning aid—not a personal medical clearance. Clearance to fly is individualized: your surgeon and care team evaluate wound status, drains (if used), mobility, pain control, and other factors. Do not treat a blog timeline—or a friend’s timeline—as your ticket authorization.

## What tends to lengthen the stay

Stay plans often extend when:

• A tummy tuck is part of the combination and early mobility is more limited
• Drains are still in place or recently removed
• Swelling, bruising, or discomfort is higher than expected in the first days
• The flight home is long-haul (for example, Cartagena to many U.S. cities)
• You would be lifting children, luggage, or doing stairs without help soon after landing
• An in-person check is scheduled before travel clearance

Shorter stays may be more realistic for narrower procedure sets and short flights—but a full mommy makeover is rarely a “weekend recovery” trip. If your schedule only allows a few days after surgery, discuss whether staging procedures or adjusting travel dates is safer than compressing everything.

## A practical way to build your calendar

**Before surgery:** Ask your surgical team how early you should arrive so you can rest, complete any pending labs or evaluations, and settle into lodging close to follow-up care. Rushing from airport to OR the same day is a common planning mistake—confirm the preferred arrival window with the clinic rather than assuming a fixed number of days.

**Surgery day through the early days:** Expect limited mobility and help from a companion for walking, meals, medication timing, and clinic contact. Compression garments or support bras, if recommended, should be used exactly as your surgeon directs—not by a generic online schedule. This window is for relative rest, not tourism.

**Through the first week and beyond:** Short walks as approved, wound care, garment use, and scheduled checks. Ask when drains (if any) are typically reassessed and what warning signs require urgent contact. ISAPS and ASPS materials both emphasize following your surgeon’s individualized instructions rather than a one-size calendar.

**Toward travel clearance:** Confirm in writing or in a documented call: whether you are cleared to fly, any seat or movement guidance, garment rules for the flight, medication plans, and how remote follow-up works after you return to the U.S.

## Flight home: questions worth asking before you book

ISAPS notes that long periods of sitting—on airplanes or in cars—can raise the risk of deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism, and that those concerns are compounded after surgery. Ask your care team—not only a booking agent—about:

• Minimum days in Cartagena before considering a flight
• Whether long-haul travel changes that recommendation
• Compression garment and walking guidance during the flight
• When you can lift a carry-on or a child
• What symptoms mean you should delay travel
• How to reach the team after you are back home (photos, video, WhatsApp/clinic channel)

Buy flexible return options when possible until clearance is clearer. Changing a ticket is usually easier than managing a difficult flight too soon.

## Companion, lodging, and return-to-life after the U.S. landing

ASPS recovery education for mommy makeover patients stresses arranging help with childcare, household tasks, and daily activities in the early period. A companion also helps with logistics that are hard alone after abdominal and breast procedures: getting in and out of bed, preparing light meals, managing garments, and accompanying you to checks. Lodging with an elevator, air conditioning, and a quiet room often beats a lively hotel far from the clinic.

After you land in the U.S., recovery continues. Desk work may return sooner than lifting toddlers, exercise, or housework. Ask for written guidance on activity stages so your home schedule matches clinical advice—not social media timelines.
