If you are weighing **rhinoplasty in Colombia**, the city matters less than three decisions: who operates (and whether you can check it), whether your goal fits the nose you actually have, and how long you stay. The stay has a firm floor. Avianca, Colombia's flag carrier, permits travel 10 days after rhinoplasty or septoplasty and requires a medical certificate issued no earlier than 10 days before the flight. ASPS and the CDC suggest waiting 7 to 10 days after facial procedures. Your real flight date is the later of the airline minimum and the day your surgeon examines you in person and clears you.

Travel rules and how to check a clinic before you commit are in [choosing plastic surgery in Colombia](/plastic-surgery-colombia/). The other date to plan around is a year away. ASPS notes that a new nasal contour can take up to a year to fully refine, so the final shape of a **nose job in Colombia** is judged at home, not in Cartagena. Follow-up, and what would happen if a small revision were ever needed, belongs in the plan before you buy a ticket.

## What the consultation has to settle first

A sound rhinoplasty plan starts with your nose, not with a saved photo of someone else's. Mayo Clinic describes the meeting before surgery as a review of your goals and medical history (including nasal blockages and earlier surgery), an examination of the inside and outside of the nose, photographs from several angles, and an honest talk about what the operation can and cannot do. The exam matters because, in Mayo's words, features such as "the thickness of your skin or the strength of the cartilage at the end of your nose, may affect your results," and because it shows how surgery will change your breathing.

The Colombian Society of Plastic Surgery (SCCP) adds points that come up in almost every rhinoplasty consult:

- **Skin sets limits.** Thick skin over the tip can be improved, but it cannot be made extremely delicate without causing damage.
- **The nose is judged with the face.** It is studied together with the upper jaw, the lower jaw and the chin, and a *perfilograma* (a profile X-ray of the face used for measurements) is often requested. Mayo makes a related point: a small chin can create the illusion of a larger nose.
- **Good candidates work with their features.** SCCP describes the best candidates as people who understand their own facial type and want to improve it without demanding perfection or erasing their ethnic features.
- **Breathing counts.** A deviated septum can be straightened in the same operation, which is often called septorhinoplasty. Congestion caused only by allergies is a separate problem.

If you live abroad, the first conversation can be virtual. Send clear front and profile photos, mention any earlier nasal surgery or injury, and say plainly what bothers you. The surgical plan is still confirmed in person, with a physical examination, before surgery.

## Who should operate, and how to check in Colombia

According to Mayo Clinic, most rhinoplasties are performed by plastic surgeons, facial plastic surgeons or otolaryngologists (ENTs). It suggests looking for formal training in one of those specialties, a surgeon who does rhinoplasty often, and an accredited surgical facility or hospital. A search for rhinoplasty in any Colombian city returns more than one of these specialties, so ask which one you are dealing with and check it.

What you can verify before you travel:

1. **The national registry.** In May 2026 the SCCP urged patients to confirm that the surgeon has formal training in the procedure offered and is listed in ReTHUS, Colombia's national registry of health professionals.
2. **Society membership, if it is claimed.** SCCP membership can be checked in the society's [surgeon search](https://cirugiaplastica.org.co/buscar-cirujano/).
3. **The facility.** The same SCCP recommendations say surgery belongs in a clinic or health institution authorized (*habilitada*) by the health authorities, never in an apartment or private home. Ask for the facility's name and address in writing; it is often not the consultation address.
4. **Anesthesia.** Ask who provides it and what type is planned. Mayo notes that rhinoplasty can be done with local anesthesia plus sedation or with general anesthesia, depending on complexity.
5. **Real conversations with the surgeon.** The NHS advises two consultations with the surgeon who will operate, and asking how many procedures like yours they have done.

Dr. Fulvio Correa makes the same point in his rhinoplasty video: "when you're choosing the professional to perform a rhinoplasty, it's very important that you make sure that he or she is qualified and has the necessary experience to understand what you want to achieve."

## How many days to plan in Colombia

No published number fits everyone, but the sources agree on the milestones. Build the trip from them, then let the surgical team set your actual dates.

| Milestone | What the source says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Before surgery | In-person evaluation in Cartagena after arrival | The practice's [international patients program](/international-patients/) |
| Days 1–7 | Internal bandages or soft splints usually stay 1 to 7 days | Mayo Clinic |
| About day 7 | External splint usually in place for about one week | Mayo Clinic |
| Days 7–10 | Suggested wait before flying after facial procedures, rhinoplasty included | ASPS, CDC Yellow Book, NHS |
| Day 10 | Travel permitted after rhinoplasty and/or septoplasty | Avianca |
| Day 14 | Travel permitted after cosmetic facial surgery (a separate line in the same policy) | Avianca |
| Day 15 | Facelift or two or more facial procedures combined, depending on recovery | Avianca |
| Last 10 days before the flight | Medical certificate stating you are fit to travel | Avianca |

Turn that into a booking rule:

1. **Count from the surgery date,** not from your arrival.
2. **Ask which Avianca line applies.** The policy lists rhinoplasty and septoplasty at 10 days, and cosmetic facial surgery at 14. If another facial procedure is added to the same operation, the 15-day line can apply. Ask your team which one your medical certificate will describe.
3. **Add your days before surgery** for the in-person evaluation and any tests requested, and confirm on which days the doctor sees patients before you fix an arrival date.
4. **Keep the return ticket changeable.** If the airport health authority disagrees with your doctor about the days needed, Avianca says the health authority prevails.

Flying a different airline or connecting in another country? Read that carrier's medical policy as well. For the full airline table and in-flight clot precautions, see our guide to [flying after plastic surgery](/blog/flying-after-plastic-surgery/).

## Cartagena is a beach city. Your nose will not be on vacation

Recovering in a Caribbean beach city puts the sun and the sea right outside your door, and the sources are specific about both:

- **Sun.** SCCP advises no sun exposure for 6 weeks and sunscreen for 3 to 6 months. Mayo Clinic recommends SPF 30 on the nose whenever you are outside and warns that too much sun may cause permanent discoloration of the nasal skin.
- **Sunglasses.** Mayo says not to rest eyeglasses or sunglasses on the nose for at least four weeks. Tape them to your forehead or use cheek rests until then.
- **Water, alcohol and tours.** ASPS tells cosmetic-surgery travelers not to sunbathe, drink alcohol, swim or snorkel, or take extensive walking or bus tours after surgery. The CDC adds that these vacation activities can delay or impede healing.
- **Effort.** SCCP advises avoiding strenuous physical activity for 2 to 3 weeks and contact sports for the first 3 months.

In practice, that means packing a wide-brimmed hat, SPF 30 sunscreen and shirts that button or zip at the front (Mayo advises against pulling clothes over your head), choosing lodging where you can rest with your head raised, and accepting that the beach and the boat trips belong to a later visit.

## Rhinoplasty recovery when you are far from home

Most of a rhinoplasty recovery happens after the flight home, which changes what you should arrange before you leave.

- **Swelling runs on months, not days.** ASPS says initial swelling subsides within a few weeks, but the contour can take up to a year to refine, and swelling may come and go, and be worse in the morning, during that first year. Cleveland Clinic says about 90% of the swelling goes away after three months.
- **Small revisions are part of the conversation.** The SCCP states that approximately 15% of cases require revisions to correct small imperfections, which it does not consider a complication. Cleveland Clinic gives a similar figure: about 15% of people need a follow-up (revision) surgery to make small changes. Mayo Clinic advises waiting at least a year before a second surgery, because the nose keeps changing during that time.
- **Your records travel with you.** The CDC recommends asking for copies of your overseas medical records in English and discussing a plan for complications with both your doctor at home and the surgeon abroad.

Before surgery, ask: How will follow-up work once I am home, and with what photos or calls? Where do I go if an emergency comes up before I fly? If a revision were ever needed, where would it happen and who would pay for it? ISAPS lists these among the questions to ask a surgeon abroad, and the NHS adds one more: how easily could you travel back?

## Red flags and the questions worth bringing

**Red flags:** a surgery date offered before anyone has reviewed your photos and history, a return flight booked before a surgical plan exists, no in-person examination before surgery or before you fly, a credential or facility you cannot check, or any promise about the exact nose you will end up with.

Questions to bring to the consultation:

1. Given my skin, cartilage and breathing, which of my goals are realistic?
2. Is the plan cosmetic, functional (septum), or both?
3. Who gives the anesthesia, and where exactly will the surgery take place?
4. When does the splint come off, and when will you examine me before I fly?
5. What should I avoid in Cartagena during the first weeks?
6. How does follow-up work from home, and what happens if I ever need a revision?

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I need to stay in Colombia after rhinoplasty?**
Plan around two floors: ASPS, the CDC and the NHS suggest waiting 7 to 10 days after facial procedures before flying, and Avianca permits travel 10 days after rhinoplasty or septoplasty, with a medical certificate issued within 10 days of the flight. Add your arrival days for the in-person evaluation, and keep the ticket flexible in case clearance comes later.

**Can I fly 7 days after a nose job?**
ASPS guidance starts at 7 days for facial procedures, but Avianca's published minimum after rhinoplasty is 10 days, and its line for cosmetic facial surgery is 14. If you are flying Avianca out of Colombia, plan on at least 10 days, confirm which line applies to you, and wait for your surgeon's clearance.

**When can I wear sunglasses after rhinoplasty?**
Mayo Clinic advises not resting eyeglasses or sunglasses on the nose for at least four weeks. Until then, a wide-brimmed hat gives shade, and glasses can be taped to the forehead or supported on cheek rests.

**Can I go to the beach in Cartagena after rhinoplasty?**
Not during early recovery. SCCP advises no sun for 6 weeks and sunscreen for 3 to 6 months, and ASPS tells travelers not to sunbathe, swim or snorkel after cosmetic surgery. Your team will tell you when sun and water are allowed in your case.

**How long until rhinoplasty swelling goes down?**
Bruising and swelling around the eyes usually fade in 2 to 3 weeks, according to Mayo Clinic. Cleveland Clinic says about 90% of nasal swelling is gone after three months, and ASPS says the contour can take up to a year to fully refine.

**Should a plastic surgeon or an ENT do my rhinoplasty?**
Mayo Clinic notes that most rhinoplasties are done by plastic surgeons, facial plastic surgeons or ENTs, and suggests choosing someone with formal training in one of those specialties who performs rhinoplasty often, in an accredited facility. In Colombia, the SCCP also recommends checking the surgeon's ReTHUS registration and that the clinic is authorized by the health authorities.
